# Contributing to the Riemann Research Program

This repository is an open research program, not a claim that the Riemann Hypothesis has been proved.

## Core rule

**No gap closed, no proof claim.**

Contributions are welcome when they are precise, reproducible, and explicit about their epistemic status.

## Useful contribution types

- mathematical derivations and lemmas;
- counterexamples or failed approaches;
- independent verification of existing derivations;
- numerical experiments with deterministic inputs;
- symbolic checks;
- operator/spectral constructions;
- literature audits;
- corrections to normalization, hypotheses, or citations.

## Evidence requirements

### Mathematical claims

State:
1. the exact proposition;
2. assumptions and definitions;
3. dependencies;
4. the derivation or proof;
5. independent checks where practical;
6. known limitations and remaining gaps.

### Computational claims

State:
1. source code or exact procedure;
2. deterministic inputs;
3. software/runtime versions where relevant;
4. numerical precision;
5. error/stability information;
6. reproducible commands;
7. whether the result is exploratory, reproducible, or independently verified.

Numerical agreement with known zeta zeros is evidence for a computation, not a proof of RH.

## Status labels

Use the repository status vocabulary:

- CONJECTURAL
- DERIVED
- NUMERICALLY_SUPPORTED
- VERIFIED
- PROVED
- REFUTED
- BLOCKED

Do not use PROVED for a result whose dependencies, domain conditions, or limiting arguments remain open.

## Adversarial review

Counterexamples, failed routes, and objections are first-class research results. A contribution that falsifies a proposed route is valuable when the failure is precisely documented.

## Scope

Issues and pull requests should stay focused on mathematical or reproducibility content. Distinguish established mathematics, external results, project-derived results, numerical evidence, and conjectural ideas.
